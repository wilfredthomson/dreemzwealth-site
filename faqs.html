FAQs

Questions investors often ask

Plain-language answers about mutual funds, SIPs, NRI investing, costs, risks and tax. If your question isn't here, just ask.

Getting started

What is a mutual fund?

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in shares, bonds or other securities according to a stated objective. A professional fund manager runs it, and each investor owns units in proportion to the amount invested. Mutual funds in India are regulated by SEBI.

How much do I need to start a SIP?

Many schemes accept monthly SIPs from ₹500 or ₹1,000, and some allow smaller amounts. The minimum differs by scheme and is stated in its scheme documents. You can increase or stop a SIP later, and most fund houses also let you pause it for a while.

SIP or lump sum: which is better?

Neither is better in every case. A SIP invests a fixed amount at regular intervals, which builds a habit and spreads your purchases across market ups and downs. A lump sum invests money you already have in one go. Many investors use both. The right mix depends on your goal, your time horizon and how comfortable you are with market swings, which we discuss after your risk profile.

What documents do I need?

Resident Indians: PAN, Aadhaar or another address proof, a recent photograph, and bank details (a cancelled cheque or bank statement).

NRIs: in addition, your passport, overseas address proof, NRE or NRO bank account details and a FATCA/CRS declaration.

KYC is completed once through a KYC Registration Agency and then works across all mutual funds.

Why do you start with a risk profile?

Before suggesting any scheme, we need to understand how much risk you are able and willing to take, and how much time each goal has. A short questionnaire and a conversation help us recommend only what is suitable for you, not what is popular at the moment.

NRI investors

Can NRIs invest in Indian mutual funds?

Yes. NRIs can invest through NRE or NRO bank accounts after completing KYC and FATCA declarations. Some fund houses do not accept investments from residents of certain countries, such as the USA and Canada, or accept them only with extra paperwork, so scheme choice depends on where you live. You are also responsible for checking the rules of your country of residence.

What happens to my investments when I return to India?

Your investments continue. You update your residential status with each fund house and with your bank, and we help you with the paperwork. Your Chartered Accountant can advise on the tax side of the move.

Costs and how we are paid

Do you charge me a fee?

We don't charge you a separate fee. We are paid a commission by the Asset Management Company on investments made through us in the Regular Plan of a scheme, and it is included in the scheme's expense ratio. We tell you the commission on each scheme we suggest before you invest. See our Disclosures.

What is the difference between a Regular Plan and a Direct Plan?

Both are the same scheme with the same portfolio. A Regular Plan is bought through a distributor and its expense ratio includes the distributor's commission, so it is higher. A Direct Plan is bought without a distributor and has a lower expense ratio, but no distributor service. As a Mutual Fund Distributor, we deal only in Regular Plans.

Risks, returns and withdrawals

Are returns guaranteed?

No. Mutual Fund investments are subject to market risks, read all scheme related documents carefully. The value of your investment can go down as well as up, and past performance may or may not be sustained in future. Debt funds also carry interest-rate and credit risk.

Can I withdraw my money at any time?

For open-ended schemes, yes, on any business day. Some schemes charge an exit load if you redeem within a set period, and ELSS schemes have a 3-year lock-in. Redemption money is usually credited to your bank account within a few working days, depending on the type of scheme.

Tax and ELSS

How are mutual fund gains taxed?

It depends on the type of scheme (equity-oriented or not), how long you hold it, and whether you are a resident or an NRI. For NRIs, tax is also deducted at source when you redeem. Tax rules change from time to time, so we coordinate with your Chartered Accountant rather than give tax advice ourselves.

What is ELSS?

An Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) is an equity mutual fund with a 3-year lock-in. Under the old tax regime, investments in ELSS may qualify for a deduction of up to ₹1.5 lakh a year, together with other eligible investments. The new tax regime does not offer this deduction. Please confirm the current rules with your Chartered Accountant.

Tracking and safety

How will I know how my investments are doing?

You receive a Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) covering all your mutual fund holdings, every month in which you transact and at least every six months otherwise. Each fund house also sends statements, and you can track your investments online through the Wealthy app. We review your portfolio with you periodically.

Is my money safe with Dreemz Wealth?

Your money never comes to us. Payments go from your bank account to the mutual fund or the transaction platform's payment system, and your units are held in your own name. Each mutual fund's assets are held by an independent custodian and overseen by trustees under SEBI rules. This protects you against misuse of your money, but not against market falls.

How often will we review my portfolio?

At least once a year, and whenever something important changes in your life, such as a new goal, a move or a change in income. Reviews check that each goal is still on track and whether any rebalancing is suitable.
